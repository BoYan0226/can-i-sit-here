Can I Sit Here?

Can I Sit Here? began with a simple habit: noticing where people sit while walking

through the city. At first, I photographed chairs. Then I began to notice the

steps, low walls, and edges of planters that people used in the same way. These

everyday surfaces became places to pause, even though sitting was not their

intended purpose. As I kept looking, my attention shifted from the seats to

what happened around them. People sat beside friends, gathered on steps, and

stayed to talk. A place to rest could also become a place to spend time together.

Some surfaces are made to move us along, yet people find reasons to remain.

A few minutes of sitting changes how a street is seen. The steps still lead

somewhere, but they can also hold a conversation, a pause, and a shared view.

It asks us to notice how people use what the city already offers, and to consider

those habits when designing its future. We need room to stop, rest, and connect.
